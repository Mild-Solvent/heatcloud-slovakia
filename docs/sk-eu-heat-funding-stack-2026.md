## What it says

The funding instruments that actually bear on a datacenter-to-district-heat
project in Slovakia, as of August 2026:

**Regulatory tailwind first.** Energy Efficiency Directive (EU) 2023/1791:
datacenters **>1 MW must run a cost–benefit analysis on waste-heat recovery**
and implement it unless demonstrably infeasible (Art. 26); DCs ≥500 kW must
report energy KPIs — including waste-heat utilisation — to an EU database
(Art. 12). France already made recovery mandatory >1 MW. This is not a subsidy
but it forces every new DC in the EU to at least price the thing this field
sells.

**Grant money:**

- **Modernisation Fund** — Slovakia's envelope is ~**€3.89bn to 2030** (EU ETS
  revenue, disbursed through national schemes). District heating is an
  explicit priority axis; Bankwatch's complaint that €55M of it went to *gas*
  DH projects proves both that the money flows and that the fund is hungry for
  non-gas heat projects it can point at.
- **Program Slovensko 2021–27** (ERDF/CF, implemented by SIEA). Live example:
  call PSK-SIEA-008-2025-DV-EFRR (April 2025) for energy efficiency and RES in
  enterprises, with "connection to an efficient CZT system" among eligible
  activities. There is a dedicated **state-aid scheme for efficient district
  heating/cooling investment** with a €50M per-project notification threshold —
  the heat-recovery half of a DC project fits it; the compute half does not.
- **Plán obnovy (RRF)** Component on renewables funded CZT decarbonisation but
  the facility winds down in 2026 — treat as closed for new large projects.

**Debt and technical assistance:**

- **EIB ELENA** — grants covering up to 90% of project development costs for
  district heating/cooling investment programmes (€374M given across 206
  projects to date). Exactly the instrument for the feasibility/engineering
  phase a municipality cannot pay for.
- **EIB TARGET TA** — already active in Slovakia: Partizánske used it (signed
  Aug 2024) to plan DH modernisation including geothermal and waste-heat
  recovery. Precedent that a mid-sized Slovak town can get EIB hand-holding.
- **EIB/EBRD senior debt** — both routinely lend to CEE district heating
  rehabilitation; EIB is active in Slovak grid financing (+€50M smart-grid
  loan, Aug 2026). **Slovak Investment Holding (SIH)** can co-invest
  nationally.
- **Innovation Fund (EU ETS)** — plausible for a genuinely integrated
  DC+heat-pump+storage plant as "innovative low-carbon technology", but
  competitive and oversubscribed; an option, not a plan.

## What it changes

Supports `capital` staying at 5 but with a big asterisk on
[sk-dc-heat-utility](sk-dc-heat-utility.md): the *heat side* of the
capex (heat pumps, network connection, possibly the thermal plant) can
plausibly be 30–45% grant-funded under the CZT state-aid scheme plus
Modernisation Fund, and its engineering de-risked nearly free via ELENA/TARGET.
Nothing here funds servers or white space — the compute capex must stand on
cloud/colo revenue alone. Any pitch that claims EU money for the datacenter
itself is mis-selling and a diligence-stage kill.

## How much to trust it

Instrument existence and sizes are from the Commission/EIB and the Slovak
managing authorities — solid. What is *not* established: whether a privately
owned DC operator (rather than a regulated heat undertaking or municipality)
is an eligible applicant under each specific call, and what the realised aid
intensity would be after state-aid cumulation rules. That is a
lawyer-and-grant-consultant question and is flagged as an open question on the
play.
