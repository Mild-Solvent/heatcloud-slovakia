Datacenters that heat towns.

HeatCloud builds Infomaniak-style cloud datacenters attached to Slovakia's gas-locked district heating networks — selling compute at market rates, recovered heat at gas-minus-20%, with EU decarbonisation money covering a third of the thermal capex.

01Thesis

Every megawatt-hour of electricity a datacenter consumes leaves the building as 30–45 °C heat. In Geneva, Infomaniak's D4 facility has been re-injecting ~95% of its energy into the canton's district network as 67 °C heat since November 2024 — enough for ~6,000 homes. The engineering is proven and off-the-shelf (Trane heat pumps, Siemens controls).

Slovakia is an unusually good place to copy it, for one blunt reason: Slovak district heat is gas-fired and expensive. The regulated variable component averaged €93.4/MWh in 2025, while recovered datacenter heat can be delivered for roughly €55/MWh all-in. In Stockholm — the mature market — waste heat fetches only €35–45/MWh. The spread that is a rounding error in the Nordics is a real margin in Slovakia, and the buyer is under EU legal pressure to decarbonise.

The honest framing: heat is 15–20% of revenue — a margin enhancer, a permitting asset and a subsidy unlock. The core business is a profitable sovereign cloud/colocation operation in an underserved market (~30 MW in the whole country, no hyperscaler region). The heat story makes that datacenter cheaper to build, easier to permit, and politically wanted. Infomaniak sequenced it the same way: cloud revenue first, heat second.

02How it works

The governing arithmetic is one line: delivered waste heat costs P_elec ÷ (COP − 1) per MWhth plus capex recovery — the heat itself is free; you pay only for the temperature lift. At €165/MWh industrial power and COP 4 that is ~€55/MWhth against a €93 gas-set reference. Selling at €70/MWh, the town saves ~25% and decarbonises; we keep ~€15/MWh on 53 GWh.

Why now — four clocks that all started after 2023

  1. EED 2023/1791: every EU datacenter >1 MW must run a waste-heat cost–benefit analysis and implement recovery unless infeasible; ≥500 kW must report energy KPIs to an EU database. France already made recovery mandatory.
  2. Gas shock: Slovakia lost cheap Russian transit; its heat sector is politically desperate for non-gas sources.
  3. Modernisation Fund deadline: ~€3.89bn Slovak envelope must be spent by 2030 — and is currently embarrassing Brussels by part-flowing to gas projects.
  4. AI inference demand made small-market datacenters investable again.

06Business & regulatory requirements

  1. ÚRSO heat licence (Act 657/2004, výroba a rozvod tepla) — needed only when selling heat off-site; the pilot consumes heat on-site and avoids it. Requires a qualified responsible person (odborný zástupca).
  2. Osvedčenie o súlade with the municipal heat concept for new thermal capacity ≥10 MWth — in practice, the town must want the project. Memorandum first.
  3. ÚRSO two-component price regulation — cost-audited. Structuring the €70/MWh strategy so the regulator recognises the cost base is the sharpest regulatory risk.
  4. Grid connection ≥5 MW — application to ZSD/SSD/VSD or SEPS. The national bottleneck; file at LOI stage.
  5. Construction permits — územné konanie, stavebné povolenie (new construction act), EIA screening likely.
  6. EED compliance — Art. 26 waste-heat CBA (passed by construction), Art. 12 KPI reporting ≥500 kW.
  7. Compute certifications — ISO 27001, then EUCS/SNAS track if public-sector sovereignty demand is real. GDPR residency is the sales wedge.
  8. Heat offtake contract — 10+ year heat purchase agreement with the network operator, or licensed direct supply to large offtakers.

Pool buys heat at gas-minus-25%, 3-year term. On-site consumption → no ÚRSO supplier licence. Under 500 kW → no EED reporting.
Proof
Deep Green (UK) proved the unit economics — a Devon pool cut its heating bill >60%; Octopus Energy invested £200M in the model (2024).

The pilot converts "no track record" into "the only team in Slovakia that has actually done it" — and produces the metered dataset the full-scale heat PPA and every grant application will be built on.

10Risks & kill criteria — stated up front

  • Regulatory confiscation (most likely, least visible): ÚRSO's cost-audit regime hands the heat spread to the offtaker. Kill if the pricing decree cannot accommodate the model — checked by legal opinion in month 1, before incorporation.
  • Grid queue: ≥5 MW connections exceeding 4 years at every screened site makes the full build a 2030 story. Kill (full build) if so; wedge survives.
  • Thin compute demand: sovereignty narrative ≠ purchase orders. Kill if <1 MW of LOIs after six months of pre-selling.
  • Heat price collapse: gas falling far enough drags the €93 reference toward €60 and the heat margin with it. Mitigant: heat is never underwritten above 20% of revenue.
  • State copies it: MHTH does it itself with state money once proven. Mitigant: speed, operating know-how, and being the JV partner they need anyway.
  • Offtake refusal: MHTH + two municipals all decline >€45/MWhth → the Slovak premium is theoretical; the whole thesis reverts to Nordic economics and we stop.
